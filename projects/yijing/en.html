At an impasse, change;

“穷则变，变则通，通则久。”Xici II: “At an impasse, things change; through change they find passage; with passage they endure.”

A situation cannot be judged outside the process that formed it. What time is it? What position do you occupy? Discern time and place before deciding whether to advance or withdraw.

Not good and bad—alternation.

Yin and yang are not moral labels. They describe night and day, contraction and expansion, rest and exertion. Drag through one day: each phase has its place.

IV · Right position

The person may not be wrong; the position may be.

The same capacity produces different results at different stages. Drag a role onto a stage, or select a role and then a position.

time × positionPlace one role in one stage

“Right position” does not rank people. It asks whether capacity and circumstance fit.

VII · First principles

The I Ching studies judgment within change.

It does not fix a person or situation in place. It asks: how is this changing, what stage has arrived, what position do you occupy, and should you advance, hold, or withdraw?
